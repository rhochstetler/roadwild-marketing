Camping in Alaska

433 places to stay, each with its nearest town and where our information comes from.

Alaska's list looks different from other states. Almost half of it is official agency records, and a lot of those are Forest Service public-use cabins in the Tongass around Petersburg, Sitka and Wrangell. You reserve them ahead and most are reached by boat or floatplane, not by road.

If you're driving, the listings near Seward, Cooper Landing and Whittier on the Kenai are the ones you can pull into. Around Paxson, the sites are small river camps, better suited to a float trip than a vehicle. Distances are long and services are thin between towns, so plan fuel and water with that in mind.
