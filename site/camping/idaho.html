Camping in Idaho

526 places to stay, each with its nearest town and where our information comes from.

Stanley is a tiny town with the Sawtooth Mountains standing right behind it, sharp and close enough that they look painted on. On a calm morning at Redfish Lake just south of town, the peaks show up twice, once in the sky and once in the water. Stanley has the biggest cluster of campgrounds in the state, and it's often one of the coldest places in the country overnight, even in summer.

Beyond Stanley, Lowman sits on the Payette River with hot springs along the road, and Idaho City is an old gold-rush town with Forest Service cabins in the hills around it. In the east, Island Park has campgrounds along the Henry's Fork near Yellowstone. Up north, Elk City is about as remote as Idaho gets, so fill up before you go.

Up in the panhandle, Priest Lake near Coolin has campgrounds along the shore and on Kalispell Island, where every site is reached by boat. Idaho also has a lot of old fire lookouts and guard stations you can rent for the night, from Arid Peak in the north to Copper Basin near Mackay. In the south, City of Rocks has campsites among granite towers that climbers come a long way for, and Craters of the Moon has a campground right on the lava.
