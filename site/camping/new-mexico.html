Camping in New Mexico

133 places to stay, each with its nearest town and where our information comes from.

The Gila Wilderness, above Gila Hot Springs, was the first designated wilderness in the world, set aside in 1924. A short trail climbs to the Gila Cliff Dwellings, rooms built into caves more than 700 years ago, and the campgrounds nearby sit by the river with hot springs within reach.

In the Manzano Mountains near Tajique, Fourth of July Campground is named for its bigtooth maples, which turn bright red in October. Mills Canyon Rim near Roy looks down into a hidden canyon on the Kiowa National Grassland. Red River is a mountain town on the Enchanted Circle, and McGaffey is in the Zuni Mountains near Gallup. Most of these sit above 7,000 feet, so nights are cold.

In the northwest, Gallo Campground is a mile from the Chaco Canyon visitor center, among the boulders of Gallo Wash. El Morro has a small campground below the sandstone bluff where travelers carved their names for centuries. Down south, Cloudcroft is where people go to get out of the desert heat, with Forest Service campgrounds in the pines of the Sacramento Mountains.

What the badges mean

  • Verified by a nomad A Road Wild member reported on it from the ground.
  • Agency record From an official listing: Recreation.gov, the Forest Service or the National Park Service.
  • Overnight policy sourced We have a source for whether overnight stays are allowed.
  • Access checked Someone researched how you get in. Not a confirmation that overnight stays are allowed.
  • Listed business, not yet confirmed A private campground or RV park from map data. We have not yet confirmed it is open.
