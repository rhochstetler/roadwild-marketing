Camping in Washington

1,144 places to stay, each with its nearest town and where our information comes from.

Washington packs two different kinds of camping into one state. West of the Cascades, campgrounds sit in rainforest and along Puget Sound, including the Cascadia Marine Trail: a chain of small sites you reach by kayak or small boat, not by van. East of the crest it gets drier, with national forest campgrounds up the valleys around Leavenworth, Cle Elum and Winthrop, and lakeside spots through the Okanogan and the Columbia Basin.

Many of the listings below are national forest campgrounds matched to official records. Private RV parks cluster along the coast and near towns like Port Angeles, Birch Bay and Grand Coulee. A good number are backcountry camps on trails in the Olympics and North Cascades: worth knowing about, but you carry your bed in rather than park it. Passes and forest roads close for snow, so check seasonal openings before you drive up.
